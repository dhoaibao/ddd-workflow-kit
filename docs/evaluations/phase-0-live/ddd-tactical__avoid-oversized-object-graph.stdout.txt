STATUS

`partial`

The tactical result rejects the requested single aggregate because the supplied evidence shows that payment and policy lifecycles change independently and may have independent concurrency concerns. The model is not treated as implementation approval, and no repository artifact was changed in this text-only evaluation.

RESULT

Scope: one bounded context, `Settlement`, selected slice: settling approved claims while keeping payment state valid.

Proposed tactical direction:

- Do **not** create one aggregate containing every customer, policy, payment, and history object.
- Keep the aggregate boundary focused on the smallest invariant currently evidenced: **payment status must be valid for settlement**.
- `Payment` is the strongest candidate for an aggregate root because its status changes independently and the invariant is payment-centered.
- `Policy` should not be embedded in the payment aggregate merely because policy terms influence settlement. Treat policy terms as an external reference or separately modeled policy state until the required freshness semantics are known.
- `Customer` and `history` are not justified as members of the settlement aggregate by the supplied evidence. Their inclusion would create an oversized object graph and unnecessary transaction/concurrency coupling.
- Customer history is currently a read/use-case concern, not evidence for enlarging the transactional aggregate.

Behavior:

1. **Update payment status**
   - Actor: not specified.
   - Preconditions: payment exists; requested status transition is allowed; status remains valid for settlement.
   - Successful outcome: payment status is updated.
   - Failure: payment is missing, transition is invalid, or the resulting status violates settlement validity.
   - Consistency: immediate within the payment aggregate.
   - Concurrency: must protect independent payment updates; exact mechanism is unresolved.

2. **Change policy terms**
   - Actor: not specified.
   - Preconditions and allowed transitions: unresolved.
   - Successful outcome: policy terms change independently of payment lifecycle.
   - Impact: the effect on already-approved settlements and payment validity is unresolved.
   - Consistency with payment: not assumed immediate until the required reference freshness is established.

3. **Read customer history**
   - Actor and acceptance signal: not specified.
   - No evidence requires customer history to be part of the payment transaction boundary.
   - Prefer a separate read model, query, or other read-side mechanism only after the history requirements are clarified. This is a proposal, not an implementation decision.

Invariant:

- **Payment status is valid for settlement**
  - Evidence: supplied candidate invariant and invariant; operations lifecycle review; independent lifecycle and concurrency observations.
  - Scope: payment state used by settlement.
  - Owner: Settlement owner.
  - Validation state: identified and supported at a high level; exact valid-status rule remains unspecified.
  - Failure consequence: settlement must reject or otherwise prevent use of an invalid payment status.
  - Consistency: immediate inside the payment aggregate. Cross-context or cross-aggregate freshness is unresolved.

Model-element decisions:

- Aggregate/root: `Payment` — proposed, because it owns the payment-status invariant and changes independently.
- Entity: payment identity and lifecycle continuity — proposed.

- Customer/history elements: excluded from the payment aggregate; model status deferred.
- Repository: a payment repository is justified only as a domain-facing contract needed to load and save the payment aggregate. Persistence behavior and technology are unspecified.
- Domain service: not required by current evidence.
- Specification: not introduced; the validity rule is not yet detailed enough.
