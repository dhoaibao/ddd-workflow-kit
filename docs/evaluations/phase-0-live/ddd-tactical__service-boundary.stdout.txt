RESULT

Scope: one bounded context, Claims; selected slice: approving a claim after policy verification.

Behavior:

- Command/use case: `Approve claim`
- Actor: not specified; remains an open ownership/detail question.
- Preconditions:
  - A claim can be loaded.
  - The policy condition has been verified and is available before approval.
- Flow:
  1. Application orchestration loads the claim aggregate.
  2. It invokes the claim’s approval rule with the verified policy condition.
  3. The claim aggregate accepts or rejects approval.
  4. On success, the application flow saves the aggregate.
  5. A result/approval fact is published after successful persistence, subject to delivery and retry policy.
- Successful outcome: the claim is approved only when the policy condition is verified.
- Failure: approval is rejected when the policy condition is not verified.
- Counterexample: a coordinator must not save or publish an approval result for a rejected approval.
- Acceptance signal to define: an approved claim is persisted only after the invariant succeeds, and publication represents a completed approval rather than a request to approve.

Invariant:

- `Approval requires a verified policy condition.`
- Scope: one claim aggregate.
- Owner: Claims domain/decision owner.
- Consequence: approval must fail without changing the claim to approved.
- Consistency: immediate within the claim aggregate transaction.
- Validation: supported by the supplied candidate invariant, invariant, assumption, and claims policy review; the exact failure behavior remains partially unresolved.

Model-element decisions:

- Aggregate: proposed `Claim` aggregate, because the named invariant is a claim decision invariant requiring immediate consistency.
- Aggregate root: `Claim`; external callers access approval through the root rather than changing approval state directly.
- Entity: `Claim`, requiring identity and continuity across approval attempts.
- Value object: no additional value object is required by the supplied evidence. The verified policy condition may be represented as an input value or policy result, but its structure and validation are not yet evidenced.
- Domain behavior: approval belongs on the `Claim` aggregate because the evidence states that the policy rule belongs to the claim decision.
- Application service/orchestrator: proposed for the load–invoke–save–publish flow. This is orchestration, not a domain service.
- Domain service: not needed on current evidence. The rule is owned by the claim decision and can be protected by the aggregate root.
- Repository: proposed `ClaimRepository` only as a domain-facing persistence contract for loading and saving the `Claim` aggregate. Persistence technology and lookup details are unspecified.
- Factory: not needed; no construction complexity is evidenced.
- Specification: not needed; the invariant can be expressed as aggregate behavior using the verified policy condition.
- Domain event: a past-tense approval fact, such as `ClaimApproved`, is proposed only to communicate the completed result. It is not a command and does not imply event sourcing.

Consistency and integration assumptions:

- The approval invariant is immediately consistent inside the claim aggregate transaction.
- Policy supplies a verification outcome before approval, as stated in the assumption and relationship.
- Policy verification is treated as an input/result dependency, not as an invariant spanning two aggregates.
- Persistence must complete before the approval result is published.
- Delivery, ordering, duplication, idempotency, retry, recovery, and privacy behavior are not sufficiently evidenced and require definition.
- Publishing a result must not cause approval to occur twice; the delivery mechanism or consumer contract must define idempotency.
- CQRS: `not-needed` on current evidence; no read/write separation need is established.
- Event sourcing: `not-needed`; ordinary domain events are sufficient if publication is required.
